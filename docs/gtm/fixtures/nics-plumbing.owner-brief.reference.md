# 1. What we are building

Seattle has plenty of plumbers. It does not have a plumber anyone can describe in one sentence. That is the opening, and it is sitting there right now.

We are going to make you the plumber for **older Seattle homes**, starting in Queen Anne.

> ## Old Homes. Modern Plumbing.
>
> **Plumbing repair, water heaters, repiping and sewer service for Queen Anne and nearby Seattle neighborhoods.**
>
> ### Nic, the Classic Home Plumber

Not the expensive plumber. Not the fancy plumber. **The one people call when they care about the house.**

That is a reason to remember you, and it is a reason to choose you over someone cheaper.

### The one thing this must not do

A specialty can quietly scare off the everyday work that fills a new plumber's week. Somebody in a 2006 townhouse with a dead water heater should never read your site and think you are not for them.

So every page and every ad carries this line underneath:

> **All residential plumbing, any Seattle home. We just happen to be very good at the old ones.**

The old homes are why they remember you. The everyday work is why they call today. You need both.

# 2. Why this will work

**Nobody else has claimed it.** We measured the three established plumbers around Queen Anne. None of them treats the neighborhood as home turf, none of them shows pricing, and the most established Queen Anne name has 2.8 stars from five reviews on a Google profile nobody has claimed. That is not a strong position to defend.

**The houses are there.** Seattle as a whole is about 23% pre-1940 homes. The neighborhoods we are pointing you at run closer to 36%, with far more owner-occupied single-family houses. North Ballard is the single strongest fit in the city. This is US Census data, not a guess.

**Two rule changes just landed in your favor.** Water heater replacement permits became mandatory across King County on July 1, 2026, and Seattle side sewer permits moved to a different city department last year. Almost none of your competitors' websites have caught up. "We pull the permit" is now something a homeowner can check, and you will be the one who is right.

**You already look the part.** The logo, the truck, the uniform: that work is done and it is good. Most new plumbers are starting from nothing here.

# 3. Who you are for

> **A homeowner in Queen Anne or nearby who owns an older, well-kept house and cares more about the job being done right than about finding the cheapest plumber.**

They will pay more for care, communication and confidence. They will not pay more for a logo.

What they actually worry about: protecting hardwood, plaster, tile and cabinetry. Not having to call someone back for the same problem. Understanding what they are paying for. Knowing when you will arrive. Letting a stranger into a house full of original finishes.

### Who you are not for

Naming this keeps the brand honest.

- **Lowest-bid shoppers.** You will lose these, and you should.
- **Flippers and absentee investors.** Price and speed only, no interest in doing it properly.
- **Large apartment and commercial buildings.** Different licensing, different scheduling, different sale.

# 4. Where you work first

The instinct is to take work anywhere in Seattle. Resist it for the first year. Winning one small area completely is the only affordable way in, because the citywide search terms are already owned by Roto-Rooter and the directory sites and you cannot outspend them.

| Tier | Where | What it means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Core, at launch** | Queen Anne, Magnolia, Ballard, Fremont, Wallingford | This is where the money goes. Ads, content, reviews, the truck, the yard signs. |
| **Expansion, later** | Phinney Ridge, Green Lake, Greenwood | Added once the core calendar is consistently full. |
| **Take it, do not chase it** | Belltown, South Lake Union, Downtown | Mostly condos and towers. The building decides, not the homeowner. |

Work outside these areas is still good work. Just do not spend money going to look for it yet.

# 5. What your customers get

Four promises. They are deliberately plain, so a customer can repeat them to a neighbor.

> ### 1. You'll know what's wrong.
> Photos, video or camera footage on every job where it is physically possible. Nobody should be asked to approve expensive work they have not seen.

> ### 2. You'll know what it costs.
> Options shown, work approved before it starts. No surprise invoices.

> ### 3. You'll know who's coming.
> Appointment confirmation, an on-the-way text, and a photo and name of who is arriving.

> ### 4. We'll respect your home.
> Shoe covers, floor protection, dust containment, debris removed. We leave your space cleaner than we found it.

**These are operating commitments before they are marketing.** Promise 3 in particular cannot be kept by hand, it needs scheduling software. We will get that set up in the first few weeks. Publishing promises you have no system to keep is the fastest way to earn a bad review.

### Fix these before anything prints

Cheap now, expensive later.

| 6 | **The truck in the mockups is a Jeep, the truck in the photo is a van.** | Tell us which one is real before the wrap is made. |

# 8. What happens when

Nobody wins the Queen Anne map on a brand new website in six weeks. Anyone who tells you otherwise is selling something. Here is the honest shape of it.

| When | What is happening | What you should expect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Weeks 1 to 4** | Licence and insurance published, Google profile built and verified, website live, scheduling software running, wrap and yard signs ordered | No leads yet. This is the foundation. |
| **Months 1 to 6** | Ads live and tuned weekly, every job photographed, reviews coming in at two or three a week, first neighborhood pages published | **Leads start around week two or three**, from the paid ads. You start showing up on the map in Queen Anne in the back half. |
| **Months 7 to 12** | Content published steadily, expansion neighborhoods added, case studies going up | Cost per lead starts falling. Referrals and repeat work become real. Reviews pass 150. |
| **Month 13 on** | Widen the geography, add capacity | Second truck when the calendar justifies it, not before. |

**One honest caution.** The day you hire a second technician, all four promises depend on somebody who is not you. Write the standards down before the hire, not after.
